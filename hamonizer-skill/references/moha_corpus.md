## 蛤三篇 TIER 1 — Core canonical phrases

These are the most recognizable references. Use these first.

---

### 篇一 — HK Journalist Incident (Oct 27, 2000)

**Source:** Hong Kong Cable TV footage; Ming Pao full transcript, Oct 28, 2000.

The highest-density Moha source. Jiang confronted Hong Kong journalists, code-switched to English mid-speech, and produced the most memeable moments of his career.

---

**"too young, too simple, sometimes naïve"**
- **Language:** English (interjected into Mandarin speech)
- **Original context:** Jiang lecturing Hong Kong journalists who asked about political succession: "你们呀，too young, too simple, sometimes naïve!"
- **Moha usage:** The quintessential put-down. Use when the text describes someone underestimating a complex situation, or overconfidently making a naive claim.
- **Subtle:** "The reviewer's objection, one suspects, reflects a certain youthful simplicity about how data reduction actually works."
- **Moderate:** "Too young, too simple — the paper's anonymous Referee 2 might charitably be described this way."
- **Note:** "too simple, sometimes naïve" is the most-used subset. The full phrase lands harder.

---

**"I'm angry!"**
- **Language:** English (shouted at journalists as they were escorted out)
- **Original context:** Security moved in on the journalists; Jiang said "I'm angry!" in English before returning to Mandarin.
- **Moha usage:** Moments of righteous indignation, especially when the indignation is slightly absurd or disproportionate to the context.
- **Subtle:** "One finds oneself, reading the third revision of this paragraph, simply angry."
- **Moderate:** "I'm angry! — a sentiment most astronomers have felt reviewing a 2am deadline extension."
- **Note:** The English exclamation inside a Chinese context is the whole joke. In English prose, it requires more care — pair it with something formally incongruous.

---

**长者 (zhǎng zhě) — "the elder"**
- **Language:** Chinese
- **Original context:** Jiang referred to himself as 长者 during the journalist exchange, invoking seniority as authority.
- **Moha usage:** Referring to a senior figure with mock reverence, or deploying the word in any context where seniority is being used as a trump card.
- **Subtle (English):** "the elder" or "the elder statesman" in a context where it sounds natural but slightly overdone
- **Subtle (Chinese):** 长者 in a sentence about a senior colleague or PI
- **Moderate:** 长者曰 / "as the elder observed" — clearly signals the Moha register
- **Note:** Works better in Chinese prose. In English, "the elder" needs careful placement to avoid sounding generic.

---

**悶聲大發財 / 闷声发大财 (mèn shēng fā dà cái)**
- **Language:** Chinese (Cantonese-origin proverb)
- **Original context:** Jiang cited this Cantonese proverb during the journalist incident: make big money in silence, don't attract attention.
- **Moha usage:** Any context involving strategic discretion, keeping a low profile while advancing, or advising someone not to make noise.
- **Subtle (English):** "make money in silence" or "prosper quietly" — sounds like generic business advice
- **Moderate (Chinese):** 悶聲大發財 cited directly
- **English prose note:** The English rendering loses much of the Moha texture. Recommend Chinese original with parenthetical, or save for Chinese prose.

---

**谈笑风生 (tán xiào fēng shēng)**
- **Language:** Chinese
- **Original context:** Jiang described his rapport with Mike Wallace: "我跟他谈笑风生" — "I conversed with him in a relaxed, lively manner."
- **Moha usage:** Describing a pleasant, charming exchange — especially one where the speaker is slightly too self-congratulatory about how well the conversation went.
- **Subtle:** 谈笑风生 placed in a description of a smooth collaboration or meeting
- **Moderate:** Attributing 谈笑风生 to a conversation that was clearly not that smooth
- **Note:** More nuanced reference; best for Chinese prose or bilingual readers.

---

**身经百战 (shēn jīng bǎi zhàn)**
- **Language:** Chinese
- **Original context:** Jiang told reporters he had been through hundreds of battles (implying he was unruffled by their questions).
- **Moha usage:** Mock-heroic invocation of experience and battle-hardening.
- **Subtle:** 身经百战 applied to a researcher who has survived many referee reports
- **Moderate:** Literal use in a context where the "battles" are clearly academic or bureaucratic

### 篇二 — Mike Wallace / CBS 60 Minutes (Sep 3, 2000)

**Source:** CBS *60 Minutes*, Sep 3, 2000; C-SPAN Program ID 162044.

---

**Gettysburg Address**
- **Language:** English
- **Original context:** Jiang recited the Gettysburg Address (from memory) for Mike Wallace as a display of English proficiency. Also performed for various American visitors.
- **Moha usage:** The ultimate Moha flex — an unexpected, slightly incongruous invocation of Lincoln. Works when the text touches on democracy, people, equality, or legacy.
- **Subtle:** Quoting "government of the people, by the people, for the people" as if citing Lincoln, without signaling Jiang — the 蛤丝 catches the layering
- **Moderate:** Noting that someone memorized or recited this, with the Jiang angle present
- **Opening lines most recognized:** "Four score and seven years ago..." and "...government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth."
- **Note:** The joke is that Jiang memorized Lincoln to demonstrate cosmopolitan breadth. The reference works in English academic or political prose.

---

**"Much ado about nothing"**
- **Language:** English (interpreter rendering)
- **Original context:** Barbara Walters / ABC 20/20, May 1990. Jiang described international reaction to the 1989 Tiananmen crackdown as "much ado about nothing."
- *(Note: technically from 篇外/source 04, not 蛤三篇, but widely known as Moha canon)*
- **Moha usage:** Dismissing criticism or controversy — especially when the dismissal is clearly inadequate to the situation.
- **Subtle:** Used naturally as a Shakespeare idiom; the Moha context is implicit
- **Moderate:** Placed in a sentence where the dismissal is obviously ironic or underscaled
- **Note:** Works extremely well in English prose because it functions as a legitimate idiom.

### 篇三 — CUEC / 国机二院 Visit (Apr 23, 2009)

**Source:** Video footage; The China Project, Nov 30, 2022.

---

**"Exciting!" / 亦可赛艇 / 一颗赛艇 (yì kě sài tǐng)**
- **Language:** English exclamation, phonetically rerendered in Chinese internet culture
- **Original context:** Jiang exclaimed "Exciting!" (some sources: "Excited!") upon receiving a gift from the China United Engineering Corporation.
- **Moha usage:** Moments of genuine or mock enthusiasm. "亦可赛艇" is the Chinese phonetic meme form, used as a comment-section reaction.
- **Subtle (English):** "Exciting!" used as a standalone exclamation in a context where it's slightly incongruous with the formal tone
- **Moderate (Chinese):** 亦可赛艇 or 一颗赛艇 as a reaction
- **Moderate (bilingual):** "亦可赛艇 — as it were" in English prose
- **Note:** The phonetic form (亦可赛艇) is more recognizable to 蛤丝 than the English original in Chinese prose.

---

**苟利国家生死以，岂因祸福避趋之**
- **Language:** Classical Chinese (Lin Zexu poem, 1842)
- **Original context:** Jiang cited this Lin Zexu couplet during the CUEC visit as a statement of principled duty. The second line: "Would I let fortune or misfortune determine my path?"
- **Moha usage:** Invoking principled self-sacrifice — especially in contexts where the "sacrifice" is modest (an inconvenient deadline, a difficult referee, a thankless committee role).
- **Subtle:** Quoting it in a discussion of scientific duty or career sacrifice — it functions as a legitimate classical allusion
- **Moderate:** Quoting it for something trivially inconvenient, letting the irony do the work
- **Full couplet:** 苟利国家生死以，岂因祸福避趋之
- **Note:** The classical register makes this work in formal Chinese prose. In English, a translation ("If it serves the nation, I will give my life; shall I be deterred by fortune or misfortune?") carries the Moha signal only for readers who know the source.

## TIER 2 — Well-known but secondary references

---

**三个代表 (Three Represents)**
- **Source:** Official CCP doctrine, Feb 2000
- **Original:** 中国共产党要始终代表中国先进生产力的发展要求，代表中国先进文化的前进方向，代表中国最广大人民的根本利益。
- **Moha usage:** Mock-bureaucratic invocation of Party formulations. Use when the text has any governance, representation, or legitimacy framing.
- **Subtlety:** Harder to use subtly in English; works better in Chinese prose with readers who know the political context.

---

**O Sole Mio (Italian)**
- **Source:** Multiple occasions; documented duet with Pavarotti, 2001.
- **Moha usage:** The Italian-singing Chinese leader — the most cosmopolitan flex in Moha culture.
- **Note:** Difficult to weave into prose naturally. Best as an explicit reference ("In the manner of one who once sang O Sole Mio with Pavarotti..."). Use rarely.

---

**Moscow Nights / Подмосковные вечера**
- **Source:** Soviet-era song; Jiang sang it on state visits to Russia.
- **Moha usage:** Soviet nostalgia, Sino-Russian relations, or multilingual performance angle.
- **Note:** Even harder to place than O Sole Mio. Reserve for contexts explicitly about Russia or international relations.

## Fabrications — do not use

These circulate online but are not in the verified corpus:
- Leaked internal speech quotes without named publication
- Social media posts attributed to Jiang
- Any quote not traceable to a named primary source event
